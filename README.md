<h1>Pole Climbing Robot</h1>

A compact robotic system designed for vertical pole climbing, surveillance, and remote inspection applications.

<h1>Overview</h1>

The Pole Climbing Robot is a mechanically driven robotic system developed to climb vertical poles while maintaining stable contact with the pole surface.

The project focuses on developing a compact and reliable mechanism that can be used for surveillance, monitoring, and future inspection applications, reducing the need for humans to physically climb poles and potentially improving safety and accessibility.

The robot combines mechanical design, CAD modelling, motor-driven motion, electronics, and wireless control into a single integrated system.

<h1> Purpose of the Project </h1>

Climbing poles manually for surveillance, monitoring, maintenance, or inspection can be:

Risky for human operators
Time-consuming
Difficult at greater heights
Physically demanding
Limited by accessibility

The purpose of this project is to develop a robotic alternative that can climb vertical poles remotely, providing a platform that can potentially carry cameras or sensors for monitoring and inspection tasks

<h1> Objectives </h1>

The major objectives of the project were:

Design a mechanism capable of climbing vertical poles.
Maintain sufficient grip and stability during climbing.
Develop a lightweight and compact mechanical structure.
Integrate motors and electronics for controlled movement.
Enable remote operation of the robot.
Build and test a physical prototype.
Identify and improve issues related to slippage, alignment, and stability.

<h1> Working Principle </h1>

The robot uses a wheel-based gripping mechanism to maintain contact with the pole.

The basic working process is:

The wheels maintain contact with the pole.
Geared motors provide the required torque.
Motor rotation produces upward or downward movement.
The gripping arrangement helps maintain stability.
The ESP32 controls the motor system.
The robot can be operated remotely.

<h1> Project Structure </h1>

The project was developed through multiple stages:

1. Concept Development

Identified the need for a robotic solution for vertical pole surveillance and monitoring.

2. Mechanical Design

Designed the robot frame, wheel arrangement, and supporting components using CAD.

3. Motor & Mechanism Selection

Selected geared motors based on the required torque and climbing requirements.

4. Electronics Integration

Integrated the ESP32, motor drivers, power supply, and motors.

5. Prototype Development

Manufactured and assembled the designed components into a functional prototype.

6. Testing & Iteration

Tested the robot on vertical poles and improved the design based on issues such as:

Wheel slipping
Alignment
Stability
Grip
Weight distribution

<h1> Future Scope </h1>

The current prototype provides a foundation for developing a more advanced autonomous climbing robot.

Possible future improvements include:

Autonomous Climbing

Develop automatic climbing and positioning using sensors and feedback control.

AI-Based Inspection

Use computer vision and machine learning to identify:

Cracks
Corrosion
Surface damage
Structural abnormalities
Advanced Communication

Improved Power System

Develop a lightweight and higher-capacity battery system for longer operating time.

Improved Mechanical Design

Further reduce weight while increasing grip, stability, and climbing efficiency.

Variable Pole Diameter

Develop an adaptive gripping mechanism capable of working across a wider range of pole diameters.
